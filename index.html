About

I am a precision agriculture researcher at North Dakota State University, working in the Department of Agricultural and Biosystems Engineering. My work asks how farmers can know what their soil needs, field by field and spot by spot, fast enough to act on it.

I combine hyperspectral sensing, deep learning and ground robots to measure soil nitrogen, phosphorus and moisture in real time, so fertilizer goes only where it is needed. My path here ran through learning about precision agriculture technologies during an undergraduate research experience at Kansas State University, applying remote sensing and GIS to map irrigation demand during my master's at Punjab Agricultural University, India, and wanting to pursue research in precision agriculture using more advanced technologies.

Looking ahead, I want to expand my research in site-specific crop management and variable rate technology and serve multiple stakeholders who keep the food system running, from farmers to agronomists to policymakers.

Current research

Hyperspectral soil sensing with AI

Soil reflects light differently depending on what it holds. I train deep learning models, including multitask networks and neural architecture search, on hyperspectral images across the VNIR and SWIR ranges to predict soil nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium. I am working on developing both classification and regression models and add more properties like pH and EC.

Robots for real-time soil assessment

With engineers, agronomists and soil scientists, I am working on developing ground robots (UGVs) so they can assess nutrients and moisture as they drive the field. The goal is an autonomous soil health monitoring system to replace slow lab tests. So far we have a semi-autonomous soil sampling system.
